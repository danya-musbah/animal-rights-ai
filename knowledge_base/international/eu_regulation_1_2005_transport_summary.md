# European Union: Council Regulation (EC) No 1/2005 on the Protection of Animals During Transport — Summary

## Overview

Council Regulation (EC) No 1/2005 of 22 December 2004 lays down common EU-wide rules for
the protection of live vertebrate animals during transport connected with an economic
activity, both within the EU and for consignments entering or leaving it. It replaced
earlier transport rules and was designed to reduce injury and suffering caused by poor
transport conditions.

## General Principles

The regulation establishes several general conditions that must be met for any transport
of animals:

- animals must be **fit for the journey** before it begins;
- personnel handling animals must be **trained or competent**, and must not use violence
  or methods likely to cause unnecessary fear, injury, or suffering;
- transport must proceed **without unnecessary delay**, with regular welfare checks;
- vehicles and loading/unloading facilities must be designed and operated to avoid injury
  and ensure animal safety;
- animals must be given **sufficient floor space and height**; and
- **water, feed, and rest** must be offered at suitable intervals.

## Journey Time Limits and Rest Periods

For domestic Equidae (horses etc.) and domestic bovine, ovine, caprine, and porcine
animals, once the total transport time exceeds **8 hours**, the journey becomes a "long
journey" subject to additional requirements: vehicles must meet enhanced standards (e.g.
water supply systems, ventilation, satellite tracking), and a rest break at an approved
control post is mandatory once specific maximum travel periods are reached.

Under the current rules, unweaned animals must be given a rest period after a maximum of
around 9 hours, while other animals of the relevant species must be given a rest period
of at least one hour after longer travel periods (commonly cited around 14 hours),
sufficient to allow them to be given liquid and, if necessary, fed. Maximum journey
durations before a mandatory 24-hour rest outside the vehicle vary by species and category
(for example, figures cited in official EU sources and analyses include roughly 18 hours
for unweaned animals with a 1-hour break, and up to 24 hours for pigs and equidae under
specific conditions) — the regulation's Annex I, Chapter V contains the definitive,
detailed limits and should always be consulted directly for exact figures relevant to a
specific case.

The regulation also requires a temperature range inside the means of transport of
approximately 5°C to 30°C (with a tolerance), reflecting welfare concerns about heat
stress, which remains a documented enforcement challenge in warmer regions and seasons.

## Enforcement and Known Gaps

Multiple official EU reports (including a 2019 European Parliament implementation report)
and NGO monitoring have documented recurring enforcement problems, including: overcrowding,
insufficient headroom, failure to provide required rest/food/water, transport of unfit or
pregnant animals, incomplete journey log documentation, and weak cross-border enforcement,
particularly on long journeys to or through non-EU countries.

## Ongoing Reform

The European Commission published a legislative proposal on 7 December 2023 to revise
Regulation 1/2005, as part of the EU's "Farm to Fork" strategy. The proposal aims to
tighten journey time limits (particularly for vulnerable categories of animals such as
unweaned calves), improve space allowances, and modernise digital compliance tools. As of
the last verified information in this knowledge base, this proposal was still moving
through the EU legislative process and had not replaced Regulation 1/2005. Users asking
about "the current rules" should be told that reform is in progress and that the original
2005 regulation remains in force until any replacement is formally adopted.

## What WOAH Is

The World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH; historically known by its French acronym
OIE, from "Office International des Épizooties") is an intergovernmental organisation
responsible for improving animal health and welfare worldwide. It was established in 1924
and today has a large international membership of countries. WOAH is recognised by the
World Trade Organization as the reference standard-setting body for animal health matters
in international trade.

## Animal Welfare Standards

Since the mid-2000s, WOAH has progressively developed animal welfare standards, adopted by
its member countries, covering areas including:

- transport of animals by land and sea;
- slaughter and killing of animals for disease control purposes;
- slaughter of animals for human consumption;
- control of stray dog populations;
- welfare aspects of animal production systems (e.g. broiler chicken and dairy cattle
  production systems); and
- use of animals in research and education.

These standards are published in WOAH's **Terrestrial Animal Health Code** and **Aquatic
Animal Health Code**, which are periodically reviewed and updated by WOAH's specialist
Animal Welfare Working Group and adopted by the World Assembly of Delegates.

## Legal Status: Standard, Not Binding Law

A critical distinction for users of this knowledge base: WOAH standards are **international
reference standards**, not directly binding international law. They function similarly to
other international technical standards — countries are encouraged, and in some trade
contexts practically incentivised, to align national regulation with them, but a WOAH
standard does not itself create an enforceable legal obligation within a country unless
that country's own law adopts or incorporates it. Whether a specific WOAH welfare standard
is legally enforceable in a given country therefore depends entirely on that country's own
domestic legislation.

## Relationship to Trade

Because WOAH is recognised as the WTO's reference body for animal health standards, WOAH
codes can become relevant to international trade disputes and sanitary/phytosanitary trade
measures, giving them practical influence beyond pure voluntary guidance, even though they
remain non-binding as a matter of formal treaty law in the way the CITES convention is
binding on its parties.

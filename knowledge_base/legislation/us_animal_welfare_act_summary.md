# United States: The Animal Welfare Act (AWA), 1966 — Summary

## Overview and History

The Animal Welfare Act (AWA) was signed into law by President Lyndon B. Johnson on
August 24, 1966, originally as the "Laboratory Animal Welfare Act." It was passed after
public outcry over pet theft for sale to research laboratories. It is the primary US
federal law regulating the treatment of animals in research, exhibition, commercial
breeding/dealing, and commercial transport. It has been amended multiple times, including
in 1970, 1976, 1985, 1990, 2002, and 2007, expanding its scope and enforcement mechanisms.
The AWA is enforced by the US Department of Agriculture (USDA), through its Animal and
Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS).

## What the Act Covers

The AWA sets minimum standards of care for animals that are:

- bred for commercial sale,
- used in biomedical or behavioral research,
- transported commercially, or
- exhibited to the public (e.g. zoos, circuses, marine mammal parks).

Regulated standards, found in 9 C.F.R. Chapter I, cover housing, handling, sanitation,
nutrition, water, veterinary care, and protection from weather extremes, and vary by
species group (e.g. dogs and cats, non-human primates, marine mammals, "other" covered
animals).

## Important Exclusions

A frequently misunderstood aspect of the AWA is what it does **not** cover. The Act's
current regulatory definition of "animal" explicitly **excludes**:

- **farm animals** used for food, fur, or fiber production;
- **cold-blooded species** (reptiles and amphibians);
- **fish** and **invertebrates**;
- **horses not used in research**;
- **birds**, and **rats of the genus *Rattus*** and **mice of the genus *Mus*** that are
  bred for use in research.

This means the AWA does not directly regulate the welfare of livestock on farms, or of
laboratory mice, rats, and birds specifically bred for research — animals that make up
the overwhelming majority of animals used in US research and agriculture. Farm animal
welfare in the US is instead addressed (partially) by separate, narrower federal statutes
such as the Humane Methods of Slaughter Act and the Twenty-Eight Hour Law, plus a patchwork
of state laws.

## Regulated Entities

The AWA requires licensing/registration and inspection of "covered entities," including:

- animal dealers and breeders,
- exhibitors (zoos, aquariums, circuses),
- research facilities using covered animals, and
- carriers and intermediate handlers (commercial transporters).

Retail pet stores selling directly to the public, and most hobby breeders, are generally
exempt from AWA licensing requirements.

## Comparison Note

Despite the near-identical name, the US Animal Welfare Act and the UK Animal Welfare Act
2006 are structurally very different. The UK Act creates a broad, general-purpose duty of
care owed by any keeper of a covered animal (including companion animals) and criminal
offences for cruelty. The US AWA is narrower and more regulatory in character: it applies
mainly to specific commercial, research, and exhibition contexts, is enforced through
USDA licensing and inspection rather than general criminal prosecution, and excludes farm
animals and several other categories entirely.

# United Kingdom: The Animal Welfare Act 2006 (England and Wales) — Summary

**Jurisdiction:** England and Wales, United Kingdom
**Official source:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/45/contents

> This is an original, independently written summary of a real, publicly enacted UK statute.
> It is provided for educational orientation only. It is not a substitute for reading the

## Overview

The Animal Welfare Act 2006 is the principal piece of legislation governing the treatment
of animals in England and Wales. It replaced and consolidated a range of older statutes,
most notably the Protection of Animals Act 1911. The Act applies to vertebrate animals
under the control of humans (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish), and its two
central innovations were: (1) creating a positive legal **duty of care** owed by anyone
responsible for an animal, rather than only punishing cruelty after the fact, and (2)
consolidating a patchwork of older cruelty statutes into a single modern framework.

## Section 9: The Duty of Care

Section 9 of the Act requires a person responsible for an animal to take reasonable steps
to ensure that the animal's welfare needs are met, to the extent required by good practice.
These needs are commonly summarised as the animal's need for:

- a suitable environment to live in;
- a suitable diet;
- the ability to exhibit normal behaviour patterns;
- appropriate housing with, or apart from, other animals (as the species requires); and
- protection from, and prompt treatment of, pain, suffering, injury, and disease.

This framework closely mirrors the "Five Freedoms" concept developed by the UK's Farm
Animal Welfare Council (see `animal_welfare/five_freedoms_fawc_summary.md`).

## Core Offences

The Act creates several offences, including (section numbers as commonly cited in official
guidance):

- **Section 4 — Unnecessary suffering:** causing an animal unnecessary suffering, or
  failing to prevent it, where the person knew or ought reasonably to have known that the
  act (or failure to act) would have that effect.
- **Section 5 — Mutilation:** carrying out a prohibited procedure that interferes with the
  sensitive tissue or bone structure of an animal, subject to limited exceptions.
- **Section 6 — Docking of dogs' tails:** a specific prohibition (with narrow exceptions
  for certain working dog breeds, subject to conditions) reflecting Parliament's particular
  concern about this practice.
- **Section 7 — Administration of poisons.**
- **Section 8 — Animal fighting:** offences connected with organising, attending, or
  facilitating animal fights.
- **Section 9 — Duty of person responsible for an animal to ensure welfare** (see above).

## Sentencing

When originally enacted in 2006, the maximum penalty for offences under sections 4–9 was
six months' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine, and these were "summary only" offences
(triable only in a Magistrates' Court). This was widely criticised as too lenient for the
most serious cases of deliberate cruelty.

The **Animal Welfare (Sentencing) Act 2021**, which received Royal Assent on 29 April 2021
and came into force on 29 June 2021, substantially increased the maximum penalty for
offences under sections 4–8 of the 2006 Act to **five years' imprisonment and/or an
unlimited fine**, and made these offences triable "either way" (in either a Magistrates'
Court or the Crown Court, depending on severity). Section 9 (the duty-of-care offence)
remains a summary-only offence with a lower maximum penalty. Courts may also impose
disqualification orders preventing a convicted person from keeping animals.

The Sentencing Council for England and Wales published revised sentencing guidelines for
these offences, which came into force on 1 July 2023, to help courts apply the increased
sentencing powers consistently.

## Geographic Scope

The Animal Welfare Act 2006 applies only to **England and Wales**. Animal welfare is a
devolved matter in the UK:

- **Scotland** has its own equivalent statute, the Animal Health and Welfare (Scotland)
  Act 2006.
- **Northern Ireland** has its own equivalent statute, the Welfare of Animals Act
  (Northern Ireland) 2011, which independently set a five-year maximum sentence for the
  most serious offences in 2016 — several years before England and Wales followed suit.

A user asking "is this illegal in the UK" should be told that "UK" is not a single legal
jurisdiction for animal welfare purposes — the relevant statute depends on which of the
four nations the question concerns.

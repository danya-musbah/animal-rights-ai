# European Union: Council Directive 1999/74/EC on the Welfare of Laying Hens — Summary

**Document type:** Regulation (official summary — original text)
**Jurisdiction:** European Union
**Official source:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=LEGISSUM:l12067

> This is an original, independently written summary of a real EU directive. It is for

> implementing law.

## Overview

Council Directive 1999/74/EC lays down minimum standards for the protection of laying
hens kept for egg production. Adopted in 1999, it is best known for banning the use of
**conventional ("barren" or "battery") cages** across the EU, following a 13-year phase-out
period. The full ban took effect on **1 January 2012**. Battery cages had already been
banned earlier in some individual Member States, including Germany, Austria, the
Netherlands, and Sweden.

## Permitted Systems After 2012

Since the ban, only the following systems are permitted for laying hens in the EU:

- **Enriched (furnished) cages** — providing a minimum of **750 cm² of cage area per hen**,
  along with a nest, perching space, litter for pecking and scratching, and unrestricted
  access to a feed trough and drinking device.
- **Non-cage (alternative) systems** — including barn and free-range systems, where hens
  can move freely, subject to a maximum stocking density of 9 laying hens per square metre
  of usable area, at least one nest per seven hens, and adequate perches.

## Scope and Limits

The directive does not apply to establishments keeping fewer than 350 laying hens, or to
establishments rearing breeding laying hens (these smaller/different operations remain
subject to the more general Council Directive 98/58/EC on the protection of animals kept
for farming purposes). All egg production units above the threshold must be registered
with national competent authorities and given a distinguishing producer number used for
egg traceability and labelling (e.g. the codes stamped on egg shells indicating the
farming method).

## Economic and Trade Context

Multiple contemporaneous sources (European Parliament motions, industry and NGO
publications) note that the transition to enriched or cage-free systems increased
production costs for EU egg producers relative to conventional cages, and that the
directive did not include an EU-wide mechanism for one Member State to block the sale of
eggs from another Member State's non-compliant producers, which was a subject of ongoing
policy debate around the 2012 transition.

## Related Reform Context

Public and political pressure for further reform of caged farming (an "End Cage Age"
movement) has continued since 2012, including a 2020 European Citizens' Initiative and
subsequent European Commission commitments to review cage-based systems more broadly
across farmed species, not only laying hens. Any claim about the *current* status of such
further reforms should be checked against up-to-date EU sources, as policy in this area
continues to evolve.
